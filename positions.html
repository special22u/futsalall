Futsal Positions Explained

Four roles, five players, one shape

Home Rankings What Is Futsal? Positions Tournaments

With only five players on court, futsal cannot afford passengers — every position has a clear job in both attack and defence. Most teams set up in a 1-2-1 shape: a goalkeeper, a fixo at the back, two ala players wide, and a pivo at the front. Modern teams rotate constantly, but the roles below are the starting points everything else is built on.

Goalkeeper

Shot-stopper first, but in futsal also the team's first attacker: quick, accurate throws launch counter-attacks, and brave keepers step out as a "flying goalkeeper" to give their side an extra player when chasing a game.

Fixo (defender)

The anchor at the back who marks the opposition pivo, organises the defence and starts attacks with calm passing from deep — the team's defensive brain and its safety net.

Ala (wingers)

The two wide players who stretch the court, beat their marker one-against-one and arrive at the far post to finish moves — usually the fittest and most creative players in the side.

Pivo (target forward)

The reference point in attack who plays with his back to goal, shields the ball under pressure, lays it off for teammates arriving, and finishes chances inside the area himself.

The beauty of the system is its flexibility: good ala players drop in as a second fixo when defending, and a mobile pivo rotates to the wing to drag markers out of shape. Understanding the four roles makes any match far easier to follow — you start to see the rotations instead of just the running.
