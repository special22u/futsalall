What Is Futsal?

The beginner's guide to the world's fastest small-sided game

Home Rankings What Is Futsal? Positions Tournaments

Futsal is a five-a-side version of football played indoors on a hard court, roughly the size of a basketball court. It began in South America as a way to play the game in small urban spaces, and it grew into a global sport with its own World Cup, professional leagues and world rankings. If football is a marathon, futsal is a series of sprints — the ball is almost always in play, and every player is involved all the time.

The basics in one minute

Each team has five players on the court, including a goalkeeper. The ball is smaller than a football and has a low bounce, which keeps it on the floor and rewards close control — sole-of-the-boot touches are a futsal trademark. A match is two halves of 20 minutes of effective playing time, with the clock stopping when the ball is dead, so there are no wasted minutes. Substitutions are unlimited and made on the fly: players roll on and off constantly, which is why futsal teams press and sprint for the whole game without fading.

The court is compact — at international level roughly 40 by 20 metres — so there is no hiding place and no time to admire a pass. Goalkeepers can join attacks, flying goalkeepers are a normal tactical weapon late in games, and accumulated fouls lead to direct free kicks without a wall, which keeps defending honest. The result is a game with far more shots, touches and one-against-one duels per minute than the eleven-a-side game.

Why players love it

Ask elite footballers where their close control came from and many will say futsal. The small court and heavy touches per game build technique faster than almost any other format, and for spectators it is end-to-end from the first minute.
