FIFA Futsal Rankings Explained

How the world table is built, and why it changes

Home Rankings What Is Futsal? Positions Tournaments

Every national team in world futsal sits somewhere in the FIFA futsal rankings, an official table that orders teams by a points score. It is easy to read and surprisingly easy to misunderstand — because the number next to a team's name is not a vote or an opinion. It is arithmetic, earned match by match.

Points come from real matches

The system is based on a rating model similar to the Elo system used in chess. When two national teams play an official international match, points are transferred based on the result and on how strong each side was expected to be. Beat a team ranked above you and you gain a healthy chunk of points; lose to a team far below you and you give points away. A win over a giant is worth far more than a win over a minnow, which is why busy tournament months — when strong teams meet each other — move the table so much more than rounds of friendlies.

Matches at the biggest events carry the most weight. World Cup games and continental championship fixtures between top nations are where rankings are truly won and lost, and a surprise run deep into a tournament can lift a team several places in a single week. Quiet periods barely move anything, because there are simply no points on offer.

The usual names at the top

Year after year, the summit of the table is contested by a familiar group. Brazil, the sport's historic powerhouse, is almost always there or thereabouts. Spain and Portugal, the European giants, bring deep professional leagues and tournament pedigree. Iran carries Asia's standard with physical, disciplined teams, and Argentina — world champions in recent memory — rounds out a top tier that everyone else is chasing. The interesting movement usually happens just below them, where the next group of nations trades places constantly.
